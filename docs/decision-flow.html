How the engine decides

Every day, move the cash a household genuinely doesn’t need onto the debt that costs them the most — and be right about “doesn’t need.” This is the whole decision, top to bottom. It is deterministic: no clock, no network, no LLM, no randomness.

Read the diagram by its shape. The spine down the middle is short. Almost everything branches off it, into a reason to do nothing. Refusal is the product.

Gate · decide.py

Is it safe to look at all?

Seven conditions under which no amount of surplus justifies moving money. They apply to the funding account — the one the debit actually leaves. A stale savings balance can’t overdraw checking, so refusing on it would be superstition, not safety.

MOVE NOTHING
FUNDING_ACCOUNT_MISSING FUNDING_ACCOUNT_NOT_CHECKING CONNECTION_UNHEALTHY BALANCE_STALE INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY INCOME_TOO_VARIABLE BLACKOUT SWEEP_IN_FLIGHT

Balance older than 2 days is a guess. Under 60 days of history you cannot see a monthly bill. Income variation over 0.25 means their buffer is a rational hedge and sweeping it is worse than doing nothing.

Forecast · forecast.py

How low does checking go?

Project the funding account alone over 30 days and take the running low, not the ending balance — the user only has to be broke once. Every uncertain quantity resolves toward the end that hurts them.

Target · decide.py

Which card?

Highest APR wins. But Plaid doesn’t report APR for many issuers — and with two cards and no rates, paying the wrong one looks exactly like working while quietly destroying the entire point. So we say so instead.

NOTHING TO AIM AT
NO_DEBT APR_UNKNOWN

NO_DEBT is the happiest refusal in the file.

Reserve · decide.py

What isn’t ours to move?

available = low − buffer_floor − reserved_minimums

The minimum payment is not surplus. Sweeping it and then missing it would cause the exact late fee we exist to prevent.

THE MONEY ISN’T THERE
NO_SURPLUS

Nothing above the buffer and the reserved minimums.

Caps · decide.py

What are we allowed to move?

Cut down to the per-sweep cap, then the weekly cap, then the card balance. The engine may never exceed the user’s guardrails — only fall short of them.

NOT WORTH IT
BELOW_MIN_SWEEP

Under $1.00, the ACH risk and the noise of a notification cost more than the interest saved.

Sweep

An amount, a target card, and the reason codes that produced them — never sentences. explain.py is the only file with copy in it, so a wording change cannot break a financial calculation, and the LLM narrates from the codes rather than paraphrasing a finished financial claim.

The one rule inside the forecast

Money arrives late and small. It leaves early and large. A forecast that is right on average overdrafts half its users half the time. This asymmetry is the entire safety property.

QuantityWhat we assume
Income timingexpected date + jitter — it’s late
Income amountthe low end of its range
Income we’re under 80% sure ofnot money at all — it’s a hope
Obligation timingexpected date − jitter — it’s early
Obligation amountthe high end
Obligations we’re unsure ofstill counted — an uncertain bill is still a bill
Pending debitsalready gone
Pending creditsnot yet money
Our own unsettled sweepsalready gone — the bank may not have taken it yet
Discretionary spendthe p90 — every single day
That last row is measurably wrong, and we know it. Charging a p90 of daily spend across all 30 days compounds a per-day quantile into a horizon quantile — but variance grows with √t, not t. Measured against three years of a household’s own spending, the engine reserves more than they have ever spent in any 30-day stretch. It fails safe, which is exactly why it would have survived forever: nobody is overdrawn, nothing throws, and the product simply refuses more often than it should. It is deliberately not yet fixed — the fix loosens the forecast and buys bigger sweeps, and that must not happen before we can measure the breach rate.

What happens after — and why any of this is knowable

1 · decide.py

A prediction

“This $220 is not needed.” That is a claim about the future, and it is falsifiable.

2 · reality

The days pass

They get paid, rent clears, they spend. A real low balance happens, whatever we said.

3 · outcome.py

The bet is settled

Did they overdraft, and was it us? How wrong was the projection? How much safe money did we leave sitting there?

4 · the asset

Calibration

The empirical distribution of our own errors. Not the data — every competitor can buy that. The graveyard of our own mistakes.

This loop is the company. Moving money is a commodity; forecasting is hard but tractable. What compounds is knowing, from millions of observed outcomes, how often a forecast at a stated confidence was actually right — because that is what lets you move more money at the same tail risk. It only accrues to a system that acts and then looks. An advice product never learns whether it was right, because nothing happens.
