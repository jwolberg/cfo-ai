The bet, the engineering approach, and distribution

Autonomously manage and optimize a household’s cashflow to save on interest expense, pay down debt faster, save more, and invest what’s freed up, so there’s more left over for life’s necessities and desires.

Here's what's outlined below:

  1. The thesis underneath that promise
  2. Working software that decides when it is safe to move a household’s money and refuses when it isn’t. It runs against a made-up household, because no real money moves yet.
  3. Open distribution questions.

01The bet

The product

Autonomous cashflow optimization

Connect once. The system continuously cuts interest expense, accelerates paydown, and grows savings and investment. No ongoing user decisions. This build proves the hardest slice: moving money safely. Save-more and invest-more are the same engine, downstream.

Why automation

Advice doesn't move debt

Financial education / more information does not push people to make additional interest payments. The money has to move.

Why it refuses

Bad outcomes cost more than good ones save

One overdraft we cause costs more than a hundred correct sweeps save. We reimburse the fee automatically when we're wrong.

This is a service people want. Tally: ~$172M raised, $855M valuation, shut down 2024 on CAC, not the model, the technology, or the product. Users liked it: 4.5 stars across 17k+ App Store reviews, $1B+ in debt paid off, ~$4,300 average lifetime savings per member.
The estate split: LendingClub took the consumer app, while Pagaya took the decisioning platform, presumably to embed into partners it already serves at scale.
Conservative estimates and auditability are must-haves from day one. Every scaled competitor in this category has ended up in front of a regulator. Dave: FTC + DOJ, 2024. Brigit: $18M FTC settlement. Always stems from consent, fees, harm, i.e. a wrong “it’s safe to sweep” is a textbook UDAAP fact pattern. An an append-only decision log including the full frozen input behind every sweep is what will make refusal auditable rather than merely asserted. That log is not built yet. It is a prerequisite, not an afterthought, and the engine is already shaped for it: every decision is emitted as stable reason codes rather than sentences, so a decision recorded today still means the same thing in five years after the copy has been rewritten three times. The record exists in the right form; nothing yet persists it. Reg E, NACHA, KYC/AML, and GLBA sign-off comes before any real rail connects.
A bank won't build this — sweeping deposits onto a card it issues itself destroys the interest income it earns. Bank ROE has recovered somewhat over the past year, but the industry still trades near 1.0x book, two-thirds below the average for other industries — the market isn't underwriting the durability of that recovery, and capital allocation inside banks is fracturing by product line rather than lifting uniformly. Distribution has to run through the institutions that do have a balance sheet, not around them: B2B2C, not direct-to-consumer — embedded in a deposit-only neobank, a credit union, an employer benefits channel, or a specialty-finance network, not sold app-by-app to households.

02The engineering approach

What I built

Status: a 1-day prototype of what two screens on the customer app might look like, built and deployed at cfo-ai-1.web.app. No mechanism for moving real funds is in place — no Plaid link, no payment rail. Everything below the API is real and runs; everything that would touch an actual bank account is not built.

Below is an outline of the deployed tech stack:

LayerWhat runs
Hosting & CI Google Cloud Run. GitHub Actions: ruff, the Python suite, and the mobile typecheck and tests.
API
backend/		 FastAPI + Uvicorn. Loads the artifact once at startup and serves it from memory. No database and no write path.
Client
mobile/		 Expo / React Native in TypeScript, with react-native-web so the same two screens — the dashboard and the spending view — and the explain modal run in a browser. That browser build is what’s deployed above; the same source targets iOS and Android unchanged.
Decision engine
engine/		 Python 3.10.
Explain assistant
backend/assistant.py		 Claude Sonnet 5 — it looks up decisions the engine already made and puts them in English. It does not make them. Three read-only tools (get_decision, list_decisions, get_summary) fetch a finished decision from memory; the model has no path to move money, size a sweep, or pick a card. Every figure in its reply is checked against that turn's tool results — keyed by (date, field), so it cannot quote a real number against the wrong day, and it cannot cite a decision it never fetched. The only path that costs money, and the only one with a rate cap.
The LLM narrates, never decides. Sweeps, interest, and reason codes are computed deterministically at build time; a guard — not a system prompt — enforces the boundary. The dashboard and base explanations never call the model at all, so a provider outage can't take the product down.
What “no rail” means, precisely. Moving money here is two legs, not one. The debit leg pulls from the user’s checking — ordinary ACH origination, and a solved problem (Dwolla, Moov, Increase, Column, or Plaid Transfer alongside the link itself). The payoff leg has to land that money on a card held at someone else’s issuer, and plain ACH cannot route it — that needs creditor/biller routing, which is what Method Financial exists to sell. The second leg is the hard one, and it is the one every Tally-shaped product actually depends on. Neither is wired here.

The Decision Engine

A logic diagram for how transfer decisions are made. Note: Almost everything branches off it, into a reason to do nothing. Refusal is a feature of the product.

Input · models.py

Snapshot

Every input the engine may look at, frozen: balances, events, pending, debts, policy. today is a field, never date.today() — so a sweep stays replayable after Plaid rewrites history beneath us.

Gate · decide.py

Is it safe to look at all?

Conditions under which no amount of surplus justifies moving money. They apply to the funding account — the one the debit actually leaves. A stale savings balance can’t overdraw checking; refusing on it would be superstition, not safety.

MOVE NOTHING
FUNDING_ACCOUNT_MISSING FUNDING_ACCOUNT_NOT_CHECKING CONNECTION_UNHEALTHY BALANCE_STALE INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY INCOME_TOO_VARIABLE BLACKOUT SWEEP_IN_FLIGHT

A balance older than 2 days is a guess. Under 60 days of history you cannot see a monthly bill. Income variation over 0.25 means their buffer is a rational hedge — sweeping it is worse than doing nothing.

Forecast · forecast.py

How low does checking go?

Project the funding account 30 days out and take the running low, not the ending balance — a household only has to be broke once. Every uncertain quantity resolves toward the end that hurts them.

Target · decide.py

Which card?

Highest APR wins. But Plaid doesn’t report APR for many issuers, and with two cards and no rates, paying the wrong one looks exactly like working while quietly destroying the entire point. So we say so instead.

NOTHING TO AIM AT
NO_DEBT APR_UNKNOWN

NO_DEBT is the happiest refusal in the file.

Reserve · decide.py

What isn’t ours to move?

available = low − buffer_floor − reserved_minimums

The minimum payment is not surplus. Sweeping it and then missing it causes the exact late fee we exist to prevent.

THE MONEY ISN’T THERE
NO_SURPLUS

Nothing above the buffer and the reserved minimums.

Cadence · decide.py

Is it simply too soon?

Sweeps are spaced a week apart — a risk control, not a UX choice; every sweep is an independent draw on the tail. Daily was inherited from an earlier prototype, nobody re-examined it. Measured: daily is worth ~$36/yr in interest timing, and only pays for itself if the per-sweep overdraft rate stays under ~1% (~0.1% at a realistic all-in cost). We now have a bound — 0 overdrafts in 590 sweeps, so ≤0.51% — which rules out the first and cannot yet exclude the second. The cadence stays weekly. Sits after the forecast, not the gates: a held day still carries its projected low and stays gradeable.

NOT YET
CADENCE_HOLD

“We paid an extra $1,600.00 for you 1 day ago. Our next check-in is in 6 days.” What they got, and when we’re back — not a lecture about our cadence policy.

Caps · decide.py

What are we allowed to move?

Cut to the per-sweep cap, then the weekly cap, then the card balance. The engine may never exceed the user’s guardrails — only fall short of them.

NOT WORTH IT
BELOW_MIN_SWEEP

Under $1.00, the ACH risk and the noise of a notification cost more than the interest saved. Still indefensible at any cadence, and deliberately left alone — it is its own money-rule change.

Output

Sweep

An amount, a target card, and the reason codes that produced them — never sentences. explain.py holds the only copy in the system, so a wording change cannot break a calculation, and the LLM narrates from the codes rather than paraphrasing a finished financial claim.

The one rule inside the forecast

Before it moves a dollar, the engine has to answer one question: how low does this household’s checking account get over the next 30 days? That is a guess about the future, built out of a dozen smaller guesses — when the paycheck lands, how big rent is, what they’ll spend on a Saturday. Every one of them is deliberately resolved against us.

Money arrives late and small. It leaves early and large.
A forecast that is right on average overdrafts half its users half the time.

What happens after the money moves

1 · decide.py

A prediction

“This $220 is not needed.” A claim about the future — and a falsifiable one.

2 · reality

The days pass

They get paid, rent clears, they spend. A real low balance happens, whatever we said.

3 · outcome.py

The bet is settled

Did they overdraft, and was it us? How wrong was the projection? How much safe money did we leave on the table?

4 · the asset

Calibration

The distribution of our own errors. Not the data — any competitor can buy that. The graveyard of our own mistakes.

This loop is the company. Moving money is a commodity. What compounds is knowing, across millions of outcomes, how often a stated confidence was actually right — that's what lets more money move at the same tail risk. Only a system that acts and then checks accrues it; advice never finds out.

EXAMPLE: The loop is closed and it has run. replay.py grades one household; calibrate.py grades sixty.

03Open distribution questions

This build doesn’t resolve either one. Flagging them beats the alternative, which is someone else finding them first.

Distribution. D2C is the channel that already failed once, expensively — Tally died on acquisition cost, not cost-to-serve, and cheaper tooling doesn’t move that number. The alternative is an embedded channel, or a distribution insight nobody's found yet.

One constraint rules out the obvious embed: a bank that also issues the card being paid down loses revenue on every sweep. Candidates below don't carry that conflict:

Candidate channelWhy it doesn't fight itself
Deposit-only challenger bank No revolving-card book — a sweep onto someone else's card is free goodwill, not lost revenue.
Credit union Member-health incentives already point away from card yield.
Employer benefits channel Zero revenue conflict. Slowest option here, possibly the right one.
Specialty-finance / credit network, directly Skip the consumer product — sell the calibration, not a household relationship.
Monetization. “If the math doesn’t improve net worth, we don’t ship it” rules out late fees, hidden interest, and subscriptions — which fail as businesses anyway. It doesn't specify a mechanic. A share of interest genuinely avoided fits the constraint and the realized KPI, but hasn't closed on a spreadsheet yet.
Drawn from the engine itself, and from my own reasoning about the market it's entering.
