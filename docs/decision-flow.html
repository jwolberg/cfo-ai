The bet, the engineering approach, and distribution

Autonomously manage and optimize a household’s cashflow — save on interest expense, pay down debt faster, save more, and invest what’s freed up, so there’s more left over for life’s necessities and desires, not less to think about.

Here's what's outlined below:

  1. The thesis underneath that promise
  2. A real decision engine that tests the hardest slice of it against a synthetic household
  3. The open distribution questions nobody's closed yet — the two I’d put at the top of my own list

01The bet

The product

Autonomous cashflow optimization

Connect once. The system continuously cuts interest expense, accelerates paydown, and grows savings and investment — no dashboard, no decisions. This build proves the hardest slice: moving money safely. Save-more and invest-more are the same engine, downstream.

Why automation

Advice doesn't move debt

Financial education / more information does not push people to make additional interest payments — the money has to move.

Why it refuses

Bad outcomes cost more than good ones save

One overdraft we cause costs more than a hundred correct sweeps save. We reimburse the fee automatically when we're wrong.

This is a service people want. Tally: ~$172M raised, $855M valuation, shut down 2024 — on CAC, not the model, the technology, or the product.Users liked it: 4.5 stars across 17k+ App Store reviews, $1B+ in debt paid off, ~$4,300 average lifetime savings per member.
The estate split on the CAC line — LendingClub took the consumer app, while Pagaya took the decisioning platform to embed into partners it already serves at scale, presumably.
Conservative estimates and auditability are must-haves from day one. Every scaled competitor in this category has ended up in front of a regulator. Dave: FTC + DOJ, 2024. Brigit: $18M FTC settlement. Always stems from consent, fees, harm — a wrong “it’s safe to sweep” is a textbook UDAAP fact pattern. The guarantee plus an append-only decision log (full frozen input behind every sweep) is what makes refusal auditable rather than asserted; Reg E, NACHA, KYC/AML, and GLBA sign-off comes before any real rail connects.
A bank won't build this — sweeping deposits onto a card it issues itself destroys the interest income it earns. Bank ROE has recovered somewhat over the past year, but the industry still trades near 1.0x book, two-thirds below the average for other industries — the market isn't underwriting the durability of that recovery, and capital allocation inside banks is fracturing by product line rather than lifting uniformly. Distribution has to run through the institutions that do have a balance sheet, not around them: B2B2C, not direct-to-consumer — embedded in a deposit-only neobank, a credit union, an employer benefits channel, or a specialty-finance network, not sold app-by-app to households.

02The engineering approach

What I built

Status: engine built, and now graded against a synthetic population — it has real numbers about its own error, and they have already refused one change and caught one bug. Nothing moves real money yet — no Plaid link, no ACH rail. Dependency-free engine; everything else in the stack sits around it, never inside the decision.

LayerWhat runs
Decision engine
engine/		 Pure Python 3.10+, zero dependencies. Frozen dataclasses, money as Decimal — money() refuses a float outright. No clock, no network, no LLM.
The household
sim/		 A seeded synthetic household. Spend is a zero-inflated lognormal, because the engine reserves a p90 and the whole question is about the tail. Same seed, byte-identical history, forever. No Plaid, no real balances.
Build step
backend/precompute.py		 Walks the household day by day, calls decide() once per day, and writes backend/data/decisions.json. Committed, not built at deploy — and a test regenerates it and asserts the bytes match, so a stale demo is a red build. Every day gets a decision; about one in nine is a sweep (10 of 90) — the cadence cap alone allows at most one in seven, and the other gates take the rest.
The grader
engine/outcome.py
backend/replay.py		 Settles the bet. Replays a household day by day and grades every day it can honestly grade — a blocking refusal never ran a forecast, so it is not graded rather than being scored a perfect zero. It applies the sweep to the household's own path itself, so a caller cannot forget to and quietly report the tail we'd have had if we had never acted.
The calibration
backend/calibrate.py		 Grades a population — 3 spend shapes × 20 seeds, 4,320 graded days — at every setting of the spend dial, and licenses at most one. A guardrail measured on one household is not measured.
API
backend/		 FastAPI + Uvicorn. Loads the artifact once at startup and serves it from memory. No database and no write path. Dollars cross the wire as strings, because JSON's only number is a double.
Explain assistant
backend/assistant.py		 Claude Sonnet 5, reaching the decisions through two structured tools. The only path that costs money, and the only one with a rate cap. Every figure in its reply is checked against that turn's tool results — keyed by (date, field), so it cannot quote a real number against the wrong day.
Client
mobile/		 Expo / React Native in TypeScript, with react-native-web so the same dashboard and explain modal run in a browser.
Hosting & CI Cloud Run, one worker on purpose — the spend cap lives in process memory, and a second worker is a second counter. GitHub Actions: ruff, the Python suite, and the mobile typecheck and tests.
The LLM narrates, never decides. Sweeps, interest, and reason codes are computed deterministically at build time; a guard — not a system prompt — enforces the boundary. The dashboard and base explanations never call the model at all, so a provider outage can't take the product down.

The Decision Engine

A logic diagram for how transfer decisions are made. Note: Almost everything branches off it, into a reason to do nothing. Refusal is a feature of the product.

Input · models.py

Snapshot

Every input the engine may look at, frozen: balances, events, pending, debts, policy. today is a field, never date.today() — so a sweep stays replayable after Plaid rewrites history beneath us.

Gate · decide.py

Is it safe to look at all?

Conditions under which no amount of surplus justifies moving money. They apply to the funding account — the one the debit actually leaves. A stale savings balance can’t overdraw checking; refusing on it would be superstition, not safety.

MOVE NOTHING
FUNDING_ACCOUNT_MISSING FUNDING_ACCOUNT_NOT_CHECKING CONNECTION_UNHEALTHY BALANCE_STALE INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY INCOME_TOO_VARIABLE BLACKOUT SWEEP_IN_FLIGHT

A balance older than 2 days is a guess. Under 60 days of history you cannot see a monthly bill. Income variation over 0.25 means their buffer is a rational hedge — sweeping it is worse than doing nothing.

Forecast · forecast.py

How low does checking go?

Project the funding account 30 days out and take the running low, not the ending balance — a household only has to be broke once. Every uncertain quantity resolves toward the end that hurts them.

Target · decide.py

Which card?

Highest APR wins. But Plaid doesn’t report APR for many issuers, and with two cards and no rates, paying the wrong one looks exactly like working while quietly destroying the entire point. So we say so instead.

NOTHING TO AIM AT
NO_DEBT APR_UNKNOWN

NO_DEBT is the happiest refusal in the file.

Reserve · decide.py

What isn’t ours to move?

available = low − buffer_floor − reserved_minimums

The minimum payment is not surplus. Sweeping it and then missing it causes the exact late fee we exist to prevent.

THE MONEY ISN’T THERE
NO_SURPLUS

Nothing above the buffer and the reserved minimums.

Cadence · decide.py

Is it simply too soon?

Sweeps are spaced a week apart — a risk control, not a UX choice; every sweep is an independent draw on the tail. Daily was inherited from an earlier prototype, nobody re-examined it. Measured: daily is worth ~$36/yr in interest timing, and only pays for itself if the per-sweep overdraft rate stays under ~1% (~0.1% at a realistic all-in cost). We now have a bound — 0 overdrafts in 590 sweeps, so ≤0.51% — which rules out the first and cannot yet exclude the second. The cadence stays weekly. Sits after the forecast, not the gates: a held day still carries its projected low and stays gradeable.

NOT YET
CADENCE_HOLD

“We paid an extra $1,600.00 for you 1 day ago. Our next check-in is in 6 days.” What they got, and when we’re back — not a lecture about our cadence policy.

Caps · decide.py

What are we allowed to move?

Cut to the per-sweep cap, then the weekly cap, then the card balance. The engine may never exceed the user’s guardrails — only fall short of them.

NOT WORTH IT
BELOW_MIN_SWEEP

Under $1.00, the ACH risk and the noise of a notification cost more than the interest saved. Still indefensible at any cadence, and deliberately left alone — it is its own money-rule change.

Output

Sweep

An amount, a target card, and the reason codes that produced them — never sentences. explain.py holds the only copy in the system, so a wording change cannot break a calculation, and the LLM narrates from the codes rather than paraphrasing a finished financial claim.

The one rule inside the forecast

Money arrives late and small. It leaves early and large. A forecast that is right on average overdrafts half its users half the time. This asymmetry is the safety property.

QuantityWhat we assume
Income timingexpected date + jitter — it’s late
Income amountthe low end of its range
Income we’re under 80% sure ofnot money at all — it’s a hope
Obligation timingexpected date − jitter — it’s early
Obligation amountthe high end
Obligations we’re unsure ofstill counted — an uncertain bill is still a bill
Pending debitsalready gone
Pending creditsnot yet money
Our own unsettled sweepsalready gone — the bank may not have taken it yet
Discretionary spendthe p90 — every single day
That last row is measurably wrong. Compounding a per-day p90 across 30 days grows variance with t, not √t — the engine ends up reserving more than the household has ever spent in any 30-day stretch. Fails safe, which is exactly why it would go unnoticed.
So we fixed it, measured it, and the measurement refused the fix. The replacement reserves against the household’s own worst 30-day window instead — non-parametric, no distributional assumption, unimprovable on paper. Across 60 households and 4,320 graded days it breaches 19.8% of days against today’s 2.3%, even set to their worst month ever. So the dial ships at None and the engine still uses the model we know is wrong.

The model isn’t wrong — it’s starved. It reads “their worst month” off the 60–150 days of history the engine actually has, which is only 2–5 independent months. The worst of 3 months badly understates the worst of 36 — it estimates the true worst month at 77% / 58% / 86% for the typical / fat-tailed / steady household. The bias is worst exactly where it’s most dangerous: a fat tail means the bad month is rare, so a short history almost never contains one. Given three years instead of sixty days, the same model’s breach rate falls to 3.7%, and to zero on the household it was most dangerous for. The idea was right; the data isn’t there.

What happens after the money moves

1 · decide.py

A prediction

“This $220 is not needed.” A claim about the future — and a falsifiable one.

2 · reality

The days pass

They get paid, rent clears, they spend. A real low balance happens, whatever we said.

3 · outcome.py

The bet is settled

Did they overdraft, and was it us? How wrong was the projection? How much safe money did we leave on the table?

4 · the asset

Calibration

The distribution of our own errors. Not the data — any competitor can buy that. The graveyard of our own mistakes.

This loop is the company. Moving money is a commodity. What compounds is knowing, across millions of outcomes, how often a stated confidence was actually right — that's what lets more money move at the same tail risk. Only a system that acts and then checks accrues it; advice never finds out.

The loop is closed and it has run. replay.py grades one household; calibrate.py grades sixty. The first numbers the engine has ever had about itself, across 4,320 graded days:

What we measuredWhat it says
Breach rate
days the real low came in below our projection		 2.3% — the days we were optimistic. The only number that may ever be traded for a bigger sweep.
Sweep-caused overdrafts
the guardrail that outranks the KPI		 0, in 590 sweeps. Zero events is not zero risk: that bounds the per-sweep rate at ≤0.51% (95%), which rules out the >1% world and cannot yet exclude the 0.1% one.
False-refusal cost
our conservatism, priced		 ~$544K — money we left idle that was genuinely safe to move. The prize for fixing the spend model, and the reason it is worth fixing properly.
The first thing the harness did was catch us overdrafting people. derive_cash_events emitted today’s events as future ones — but the balance is already end-of-day, so on a payday the forecast counted the paycheck twice. A $2,600 phantom inflow, a projected low thousands too high, and a sweep against money that was never there. The exact inverse of the one rule above: counting a paycheck that already landed makes it arrive twice.

Across the population: 43 sweep-caused overdrafts. The single-household demo reported zero — whether it bites depends on the cash position on whichever paydays a seed happens to produce, and one seed never landed on one. A guardrail measured on one household is not measured.

03Open distribution questions

This build doesn’t resolve either one. Flagging them beats the alternative, which is someone else finding them first.

Distribution. D2C is the channel that already failed once, expensively — Tally died on acquisition cost, not cost-to-serve, and cheaper tooling doesn’t move that number. The alternative is an embedded channel, or a distribution insight nobody's found yet.

One constraint rules out the obvious embed: a bank that also issues the card being paid down loses revenue on every sweep. Candidates below don't carry that conflict:

Candidate channelWhy it doesn't fight itself
Deposit-only challenger bank No revolving-card book — a sweep onto someone else's card is free goodwill, not lost revenue.
Credit union Member-health incentives already point away from card yield.
Employer benefits channel Zero revenue conflict. Slowest option here, possibly the right one.
Specialty-finance / credit network, directly Skip the consumer product — sell the calibration, not a household relationship.
Monetization. “If the math doesn’t improve net worth, we don’t ship it” rules out late fees, hidden interest, and subscriptions — which fail as businesses anyway. It doesn't specify a mechanic. A share of interest genuinely avoided fits the constraint and the realized KPI, but hasn't closed on a spreadsheet yet.
Drawn from the engine itself, and from my own reasoning about the market it's entering.
