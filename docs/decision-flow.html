How the engine decides

Move the cash a household genuinely doesn’t need onto the debt that costs them the most — and be right about “doesn’t need.” The whole decision, top to bottom. Deterministic: no clock, no network, no LLM, no randomness. The forecast runs every day; the money moves once a week — the cadence limits what the engine does, never what it knows.

What this is

The product · prd.md

The user does nothing

They connect checking, savings and cards once. From then on we move the cash they do not need onto the debt that costs them the most, and tell them what happened. The promise is not faster paydown — it is no longer choosing between paying down debt and being safe.

Why it acts · prd.md

Advice does not work

A 40,000-person RCT moved what cardholders chose — and seven months on, not what they owed. Information is not the missing input. So the money has to actually move; everything below exists to make that safe.

Why it refuses · strategy.md

The worst day sets the price

We sell a promise to loss-averse people, and one overdraft we caused destroys more value than a hundred correct sweeps create — we reimburse the fee when we are wrong. That is what buys the engine the right to say no this often.

What is actually running

Everything that follows is real code in this repo, running against a synthetic household. The engine is dependency-free Python; every dependency in the stack sits around it, never inside the decision.

The household
sim/		 A seeded synthetic household. Spend is a zero-inflated lognormal, because the engine reserves a p90 and the whole question is about the tail. Same seed, byte-identical history, forever. No Plaid, no real balances.
Build step
backend/precompute.py		 Walks the household day by day, calls decide() once per day, and writes backend/data/decisions.json. Every day gets a decision; about one in eight is a sweep — the cadence cap alone allows at most one in seven, and the other gates take the rest. The remaining days are refusals, and the held ones still carry their forecast so they stay gradeable.

The decision

The LLM is nowhere near the decision. Sweeps, interest and reason codes are computed deterministically at build time; the LLM only narrates decisions that already exist, and a guard — not a system prompt — is what enforces that. The dashboard and the base explanations never call it at all, so an Anthropic outage cannot take the product’s main surface down. What this demo does not do is move money: there is no Plaid link and no ACH rail — the arithmetic of refusing is the part worth proving first.

Input · models.py

Snapshot

Every input the engine may look at, frozen: balances, events, pending, debts, policy. today is a field, never date.today() — so a sweep stays replayable after Plaid rewrites history beneath us.

Gate · decide.py

Is it safe to look at all?

Conditions under which no amount of surplus justifies moving money. They apply to the funding account — the one the debit actually leaves. A stale savings balance can’t overdraw checking; refusing on it would be superstition, not safety.

MOVE NOTHING
FUNDING_ACCOUNT_MISSING FUNDING_ACCOUNT_NOT_CHECKING CONNECTION_UNHEALTHY BALANCE_STALE INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY INCOME_TOO_VARIABLE BLACKOUT SWEEP_IN_FLIGHT

A balance older than 2 days is a guess. Under 60 days of history you cannot see a monthly bill. Income variation over 0.25 means their buffer is a rational hedge — sweeping it is worse than doing nothing.

Forecast · forecast.py

How low does checking go?

Project the funding account 30 days out and take the running low, not the ending balance — a household only has to be broke once. Every uncertain quantity resolves toward the end that hurts them.

Target · decide.py

Which card?

Highest APR wins. But Plaid doesn’t report APR for many issuers, and with two cards and no rates, paying the wrong one looks exactly like working while quietly destroying the entire point. So we say so instead.

NOTHING TO AIM AT
NO_DEBT APR_UNKNOWN

NO_DEBT is the happiest refusal in the file.

Reserve · decide.py

What isn’t ours to move?

available = low − buffer_floor − reserved_minimums

The minimum payment is not surplus. Sweeping it and then missing it causes the exact late fee we exist to prevent.

THE MONEY ISN’T THERE
NO_SURPLUS

Nothing above the buffer and the reserved minimums.

Cadence · decide.py

Is it simply too soon?

Sweeps are spaced at least a week apart. Every sweep is an independent draw on the tail the PRD says decides the company, so how often we act is a risk control, not a UX detail.

The engine used to sweep daily. Nobody ever argued for it — it was inherited from the v0 advice product, whose output was a notification. When it was finally measured, daily was worth about $36/yr of interest timing. That is the whole case for it, and the cadence is not free.

What buys it back is the guarantee: we reimburse sweep-caused overdrafts, so every debit is a draw we pay for when it goes wrong. Daily takes ~142 draws a year against weekly’s ~45 — so daily only pays for itself if the per-sweep overdraft rate is under ~1%, and at a realistic cost per incident, under ~0.1%. Nobody has measured it. The cadence is insurance against a tail we cannot yet size.

Note where this sits: after the forecast, not with the gates. A held day still carries its projected low, so it can still be graded. The cadence limits what the engine does — never what it knows.

NOT YET
CADENCE_HOLD

We paid the card recently. The spare cash is safe where it is until the next one — one good payment beats several small ones.

Caps · decide.py

What are we allowed to move?

Cut to the per-sweep cap, then the weekly cap, then the card balance. The engine may never exceed the user’s guardrails — only fall short of them.

NOT WORTH IT
BELOW_MIN_SWEEP

Under $1.00, the ACH risk and the noise of a notification cost more than the interest saved. Still indefensible at any cadence, and deliberately left alone — it is its own money-rule change.

Output

Sweep

An amount, a target card, and the reason codes that produced them — never sentences. explain.py holds the only copy in the system, so a wording change cannot break a calculation, and the LLM narrates from the codes rather than paraphrasing a finished financial claim.

The one rule inside the forecast

Money arrives late and small. It leaves early and large. A forecast that is right on average overdrafts half its users half the time. This asymmetry is the safety property.

QuantityWhat we assume
Income timingexpected date + jitter — it’s late
Income amountthe low end of its range
Income we’re under 80% sure ofnot money at all — it’s a hope
Obligation timingexpected date − jitter — it’s early
Obligation amountthe high end
Obligations we’re unsure ofstill counted — an uncertain bill is still a bill
Pending debitsalready gone
Pending creditsnot yet money
Our own unsettled sweepsalready gone — the bank may not have taken it yet
Discretionary spendthe p90 — every single day
That last row is measurably wrong, and we know it. Charging a p90 of daily spend across all 30 days compounds a per-day quantile into a horizon quantile — variance grows with √t, not t. Against three years of a household’s own spending, the engine reserves more than they have ever spent in any 30-day stretch. It fails safe, which is why it could have survived forever unnoticed: nobody overdrafts, nothing throws, we simply refuse too often. Deliberately not yet fixed — the fix buys bigger sweeps, and that must not ship before we can measure the breach rate.

What happens after the money moves

1 · decide.py

A prediction

“This $220 is not needed.” A claim about the future — and a falsifiable one.

2 · reality

The days pass

They get paid, rent clears, they spend. A real low balance happens, whatever we said.

3 · outcome.py

The bet is settled

Did they overdraft, and was it us? How wrong was the projection? How much safe money did we leave on the table?

4 · the asset

Calibration

The distribution of our own errors. Not the data — any competitor can buy that. The graveyard of our own mistakes.

This loop is the company. Moving money is a commodity; forecasting is hard but tractable. What compounds is knowing, across millions of observed outcomes, how often a forecast at a stated confidence was actually right — because that is what lets us move more money at the same tail risk. It accrues only to a system that acts and then looks. An advice product never finds out whether it was right, because nothing happened.
