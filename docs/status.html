What’s built, and what isn’t

Status as of 18 July 2026. Where each piece of the path from a bank login to money actually moving stands today:

The core is built and deployed — the decision engine, the database, the API, and the app — and the harness that measures it is live too: a grader that scores each decision against what actually happened, still running only on synthetic data. What still stands between a real bank login and a decision is the recurring-event detector (it needs real data to build against).

18 July 2026

Identity & access

A verified user (via Stytch) and a membership graph that scopes every route to the households a user may touch: every route now authorizes the household against membership, and the baked shared key is retired. Built and proven against a real Postgres — not yet deployed (it needs a Stytch project wired and the demo’s read-only session baked in).

Customer controls · first write path

The product’s first user-driven writes — a settings screen for the guardrails and the card-completeness attestation that clears the engine’s money-gate — are now built, each append-only and audited. Customer sign-in and the bank-link flow are the next rung.

17 July 2026

Ingest · Plaid transport

The Plaid ingest transport — token exchange, a signature-verified webhook doorbell, and a cursored sync that lands transactions in a table — is built and proven end-to-end against Plaid Sandbox, but not deployed.

Act · payment rail

The payment rail that moves the money is built in shadow — a two-leg design behind a single TransferProvider port. The debit leg pulls the surplus from checking over ordinary ACH (Plaid Transfer, debiting the item the ingest rung already linked; Increase is the swappable backup, ADR-0007). The payoff leg lands that money on a card at someone else’s issuer — which plain ACH cannot route — through a biller-payoff API (Method, which reaches any issuer). But the rail moves nothing: the saga runs and writes its ledger while submit() is a logged no-op, not yet proven against the live Plaid Transfer, Increase, and Method sandboxes.

14 July 2026

Core · first live release

The first release: the deterministic decision engine, the API, and the customer app — published live to Firebase Hosting so the product had a link. Graded against synthetic household data.

This is a few days into a large, complex build, and the scope ahead is almost certainly bigger than what stands today. Read this as real progress on the hard middle, not as a finished product.

The engine, the database, the API and the app with four synthetic households are live and measured. No money has ever moved. The measurement grades the code, not yet real accounts.

01The product

Autonomous cashflow optimization

Connect once. Never decide again.

The system continuously cuts interest expense, accelerates paydown, and grows savings and investment. No ongoing user decisions.

This build proves the hardest slice: moving money safely. Save-more and invest-more are the same engine, downstream.

The Plaid ingest transport is built and proven against Sandbox — token exchange, the webhook doorbell, and cursored sync all land rows in a table. What remains is deploying it, a customer link screen, and the piece Sandbox can’t help build: a recurring-event detector that turns raw transactions into paychecks and bills. Without it there are no forecast events, so a linked household would still produce no decision at all — which is why the transport rung deliberately stops one seam short of the forecast.

Act, and as of 2026-07-17 it is built in shadow. Moving money is two legs, not one. The debit leg pulls from checking — ordinary ACH (Increase). The payoff leg has to land that money on a card at someone else’s issuer, which plain ACH cannot route; the channel that reaches any issuer is a biller-payoff API (Method). Method can only pay the card from an account we hold, so the money must pass through us — meaning we hold customer funds mid-transfer. That custody role (an FBO account: Reg E, reconciliation, likely an MTL) is exactly what the docs said to avoid, taken on knowingly. The saga, the append-only ledger, the idempotency guard, the webhook trust boundary, and the SWEEP_IN_FLIGHT feedback are all built and tested. Three honest caveats remain: distribution can still delete the choice (embed with a bank or issuer and the partner owns the rail, and the custody); the rail is unproven against the live vendors until the sandbox hard gate runs (it skips loudly today); and the code moves nothing — submit() is a logged no-op, and no dollar moves until a measured tail-risk number and the compliance posture are both real.

The deployed tech stack

LayerWhat runs
Hosting & CI Google Cloud Run. GitHub Actions: ruff, the Python suite, and the mobile typecheck and tests.
API
backend/		 FastAPI + Uvicorn. Serves the decision history and the spend view. Refuses to start without its secrets, or under a database role that could bypass row-level security — a service holding bad data should not serve wrong numbers one request at a time.
Client
mobile/		 Expo / React Native in TypeScript, with react-native-web so the same two screens — the dashboard and the spending view — and the explain modal run in a browser. That browser build is what’s deployed at cfo-ai-1.web.app; the same source targets iOS and Android unchanged.
Persistence
backend/db/		 Postgres (Neon), scoped by household_id. Money is NUMERIC, never a float. The decision log is partitioned by month and stores each decision’s entire frozen input, so “why did you move $220 that Tuesday” stays answerable after Plaid rewrites the underlying history. Every table is scoped twice — at the repository and by row-level security — and the test suite proves each layer with the other removed. Defence in depth that is only tested end-to-end is one layer wearing a disguise.
Decision engine
engine/		 Python 3.10, and zero dependencies. No clock, no network, no LLM, no randomness anywhere near a dollar amount. The same snapshot yields the same decision forever, which is what makes a sweep explainable, auditable, and replayable in a backtest.
Explain assistant
backend/assistant.py		 Claude Sonnet 5 — it looks up decisions the engine already made and puts them in English. It does not make them. Three read-only tools (get_decision, list_decisions, get_summary) fetch a finished decision; the model has no path to move money, size a sweep, or pick a card. Every figure in its reply is checked against that turn’s tool results — keyed by (date, field), so it cannot quote a real number against the wrong day, and it cannot cite a decision it never fetched. The only path that costs money, and the only one with a rate cap.
The LLM narrates, never decides. Sweeps, interest, and reason codes are computed deterministically before the model is ever called; a guard — not a system prompt — enforces the boundary. The dashboard and base explanations never call the model at all, so a provider outage can’t take the product down.

The Decision Engine

A logic diagram for how transfer decisions are made. Note: Almost everything branches off it, into a reason to do nothing. Refusal is a feature of the product.

Input · models.py

Snapshot

Every input the engine may look at, frozen: balances, events, pending, debts, policy. today is a field, never date.today() — so a sweep stays replayable after Plaid rewrites history beneath us.

Gate · decide.py

Is it safe to look at all?

Conditions under which no amount of surplus justifies moving money. They apply to the funding account — the one the debit actually leaves. A stale savings balance can’t overdraw checking; refusing on it would be superstition, not safety.

MOVE NOTHING
FUNDING_ACCOUNT_MISSING FUNDING_ACCOUNT_NOT_CHECKING CONNECTION_UNHEALTHY BALANCE_STALE INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY INCOME_TOO_VARIABLE BLACKOUT SWEEP_IN_FLIGHT

A balance older than 2 days is a guess. Under 60 days of history you cannot see a monthly bill. Income variation over 0.25 means their buffer is a rational hedge — sweeping it is worse than doing nothing.

Forecast · forecast.py

How low does checking go?

Project the funding account 30 days out and take the running low, not the ending balance — a household only has to be broke once. Every uncertain quantity resolves toward the end that hurts them.

Target · decide.py

Which card?

Highest APR wins. But Plaid doesn’t report APR for many issuers, and with two cards and no rates, paying the wrong one looks exactly like working while quietly destroying the entire point. So we say so instead.

NOTHING TO AIM AT
NO_DEBT APR_UNKNOWN

NO_DEBT is the happiest refusal in the file.

Reserve · decide.py

What isn’t ours to move?

available = low − buffer_floor − reserved_minimums

The minimum payment is not surplus. Sweeping it and then missing it causes the exact late fee we exist to prevent.

THE MONEY ISN’T THERE
NO_SURPLUS

Nothing above the buffer and the reserved minimums.

Cadence · decide.py

Is it simply too soon?

Sweeps are spaced a week apart — a risk control, not a UX choice; every sweep is an independent draw on the tail. Measured: daily is worth ~$36/yr in interest timing, and only pays for itself if the per-sweep overdraft rate stays under ~1% (~0.1% at a realistic all-in cost). We now have a bound — 0 overdrafts in 590 sweeps, so ≤0.51% — which rules out the first and cannot yet exclude the second. The cadence stays weekly.

NOT YET
CADENCE_HOLD

“We paid an extra $1,600.00 for you 1 day ago. Our next check-in is in 6 days.” What they got, and when we’re back — not a lecture about our cadence policy.

Caps · decide.py

What are we allowed to move?

Cut to the per-sweep cap, then the weekly cap, then the card balance. The engine may never exceed the user’s guardrails — only fall short of them.

NOT WORTH IT
BELOW_MIN_SWEEP

Under $1.00, the ACH risk and the noise of a notification cost more than the interest saved.

Output

Sweep

An amount, a target card, and the reason codes that produced them — never sentences. explain.py holds the only copy in the system, so a wording change cannot break a calculation, and the LLM narrates from the codes rather than paraphrasing a finished financial claim.

The one rule inside the forecast. Money arrives late and small. It leaves early and large. Every uncertain quantity — when the paycheck lands, how big rent is, what they spend on a Saturday — is deliberately resolved against us. A forecast that is right on average overdrafts half its users half the time.
